Datum feature

The actual surface or feature of size identified on the part. It contains manufacturing variation.

Datum

The theoretically exact plane, axis, or center plane established from the feature using its simulator.

Datum feature simulator

The ideal counterpart used to establish the datum. A physical plate or pin approximates that geometry in a fixture or gauge.

Fixture contact

A real support, pin, or contact patch. Its location and form can affect the setup. A contact point becomes a datum target only when the product definition specifies one.

Geometry, precedence, and modeling limits

The diagrams use ideal rigid geometry. A is a planar underface normal to Z. In the hole/slot scheme, B is a round hole with its axis along Z; C is the width of an X-oriented radial slot located away from B. A constrains Tz, Rx, Ry; B then constrains Tx, Ty; C then constrains Rz. In the three-plane scheme, B is a side face normal to Y, and C is an end face normal to X. B then constrains Ty and Rz; C then constrains Tx.

The same A/B/C letters therefore produce different allocations. Cylindrical primary datums, patterns, material-boundary modifiers, imperfect surfaces, and restrained or nonrigid parts require their own interpretation. These examples omit clearance and datum mobility. Clamps maintain contact; clamp force is not an additional datum. Drawing requirements govern a production or inspection setup.

Model assumptions & formulas

Worst case = tA + tB + tC. RSS = √(tA² + tB² + tC²).

RSS assumes independent, centered normal distributions and that each input tolerance equals ±3σ. Its result is a ±3σ estimate (approximately 99.73% coverage under those assumptions), not a guaranteed bound. Correlation, mean shifts, nonlinearity, and geometric sensitivity require a more complete model.
